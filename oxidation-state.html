Oxidation number rules

Apply in order; when rules conflict, the earlier (higher) rule wins.

  1. An atom in an uncombined element (Na, O₂, S₈, P₄) is 0.
  2. A monatomic ion has an oxidation state equal to its charge (Fe³⁺ = +3).
  3. The oxidation states of all atoms sum to the overall charge: 0 for a neutral compound, the ion’s charge for a polyatomic ion.
  4. Group 1 metals are always +1; Group 2 metals always +2. Al is +3; Zn is +2; Ag is +1.
  5. Fluorine is always −1.
  6. Hydrogen is +1 with nonmetals, but −1 with metals or B/Si (hydrides: NaH, CaH₂, LiAlH₄).
  7. Oxygen is usually −2. Exceptions: peroxides (H₂O₂, Na₂O₂, O₂²⁻) −1; superoxides −½; OF₂ +2.
  8. Other halogens (Cl, Br, I) are −1, unless bonded to O, F or a heavier halogen (then positive).
  9. The more electronegative element in a binary compound is negative: Group 16 −2, Group 15 −3 (H₂S, NH₃).

Method: write Σ(count × oxidation state) = charge, fill in the known atoms, solve for the unknown.

Example: Cr in Cr₂O₇²⁻: 2x + 7(−2) = −2 → 2x = +12 → x = +6.
